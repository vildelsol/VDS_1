Section 01

The one rule that explains everything

If you understand this single sentence, most of the system stops being mysterious:

The rule

What someone owes = what you billed them − what you have actually received. Worked out separately for each villa, for each kind of account (rent or maintenance), and for each currency.

Three things follow from it, and they cause nearly every question a new user has:

  • Nothing is owed until you bill it. Rent does not appear by itself on the 1st. Somebody has to press Post month. Until then the tenant's balance is zero and it is correct that it is zero.
  • Money only counts once you confirm it arrived. Writing down that a cheque is coming changes nothing anywhere. See Section 07 — this is the single most common source of confusion.
  • Unpaid months stack up. That is deliberate: it is what makes the arrears list and the 30/60/90-day ageing work.

A negative balance is not an error. It means the account has paid more than it was billed — they are in credit, and the next charge will absorb it.

Two currencies, never mixed

Some villas are billed in Jamaican dollars and some in US dollars. The system never adds the two together — not in a balance, not in a total, not in a report. Each villa is billed in its own currency.

There is an exchange rate in settings, but it only produces the small grey "≈" figures next to amounts so you can eyeball them. It never converts anything real and never feeds a total. If you try to receipt a payment in the wrong currency the system stops you and explains why.

Section 02

Signing in and getting around

Opening the system

The records live on one computer — the HP, which sits on and runs the program. Every other device (the Kamrui, a laptop, a phone) is just a window onto it. There is only ever one set of records.

  1. On the HP, the program is already running — you will see a black console window. Do not close it. Closing it takes the system down for everybody.
  2. On any device, open the browser and go to the address for the HP, for example http://hp-elitedesk:3000. Bookmark it.
  3. Type your username and password and sign in.
If it says "Reconnecting to the server"

The network hiccuped. Wait — it retries by itself and the banner clears. Anything you were saving is retried automatically and cannot be recorded twice. If the banner is still there after a minute, check the HP is switched on and the console window is still open.

Install it like a proper program

In Chrome or Edge there is a small install icon in the address bar. Press it and the system gets its own window and its own icon on the taskbar, with no browser clutter. Do this on every machine that uses it daily. It updates itself; you never reinstall.

Your own login

Everybody who touches the system should have their own account. Every action is stamped with a name in the audit trail, and that record is worthless if four people share one login. Never do daily work as the shared admin account.

Finding your way around

The list down the left is the whole system. It is grouped into three: the day-to-day screens at the top, the financial and reporting screens in the middle, and the administration screens at the bottom. The Notifications Hub carries a number badge — that is how many things are waiting for someone.

Section 04

Setting up, in the right order

Do these once, in this order, before the system goes live. Getting the order wrong creates charges you then have to unpick.

Do this first — before anything else

Go to System Settings → General → "The books begin on" and set it to 1 September 2026. Save.

This tells the system that nothing before September is its business. Without it, entering a tenant who moved in years ago makes the system bill them for their first and last month all over again — money they paid long ago to whoever kept the records before. It would also flood the Notifications Hub with an alert for every unbilled month back to their move-in date.

  1. Set "The books begin on" to 1 September 2026, as above.
  2. Check the exchange rate on the same General tab. It only drives the grey "≈" figures, but a wildly wrong rate looks careless on a printed page.
  3. Go through all 16 villas in the Units Register. For each one set the owner and their contact details, the rent or maintenance amount, the currency, and — importantly — the day of the month the payment is due. Not everyone pays on the 1st, and this is where you say so. See due days.
  4. Enter the tenants who are already living there, with their real move-in dates. Because you set the cutover date in step 1, this raises no charges at all — it simply records who is there and since when. See Section 10.
  5. Create a login for each member of staff under System Settings → Staff accounts, and set what each role is allowed to do under Role permissions. See Section 17.
  6. Point backups at a drive that is not this computer — System Settings → Backup & recovery. See Section 18. Do not skip this.
  7. Then, and only then, post September — see Section 05. That is the first charge any existing tenant will see, and it is an ordinary month's rent. Exactly as it should be.

Payment due days

Each villa has its own rent due day and its own maintenance due day, set on the villa's record in the Units Register. A tenant due on the 15th is not counted as late on the 2nd — arrears and the 30/60/90-day ageing all count from that villa's own due day.

Set these before you post the first month. Changing it later does not go back and re-date charges already raised.

Note

Villas cannot be created or deleted — the property has 16 and the system knows them. You edit the details on each one; you never add a seventeenth.

Section 05

The monthly routine

Once you are running, the whole job is a loop. This is it.

At the start of the month

  1. Open HOA & Tenants Ledger.
  2. Top right, check the month shown next to Post charges for is the month you mean.
  3. Press Post month.
  4. Every occupied villa is now billed its rent, and every owner their maintenance. A green bar confirms the month is fully posted.
Safe to press twice

Posting the same month again adds nothing. If you are not sure whether someone already did it, just press it — the system skips anything already billed and tells you how many it skipped. You cannot double-bill this way.

Through the month

  • Take payments as they come in — Section 07.
  • Print or email the receipt while the person is still standing there.
  • Add any late fees or recharged repairs with Bill a one-off — Section 06.
  • Enter utility bills and expenses as they arrive — Section 13.
  • Clear the Notifications Hub badge.

At the end of the month

  1. Confirm any payments that were logged as expected and have now actually landed — Receipts ledger → Confirm received.
  2. Tick the month off against the bank statement — Section 16.
  3. Print the operating statement from Operational Reports and file it.
  4. Take a snapshot backup — Section 18.

Section 06

Billing: putting a charge on an account

There are exactly two ways money gets onto someone's account, and knowing which to use takes two seconds.

UseForWhere
Post monthThe ordinary monthly rent and the monthly HOA maintenance fee. Everyone at once.Top right of the HOA & Tenants Ledger
Bill a one-offAnything else at all — a late fee, a repair recharged to the tenant who caused it, a fine, an amenity fee, legal costs, a special assessment.Button on the HOA & Tenants Ledger

Billing a one-off

  1. Open HOA & Tenants Ledger and press Bill a one-off.
  2. Pick the villa.
  3. Pick the account it goes on: Rent puts it on the tenant, Maintenance puts it on the owner. Choose carefully — this decides who gets the bill.
  4. Choose the type (late fee, repair recharge, fine, and so on), enter the amount and the currency.
  5. Set the due date and write a plain description. Whatever you type here appears on their statement, so write it for them to read, not for you.
  6. Save.

A one-off behaves exactly like rent from then on: it ages into arrears, it appears on the statement, and an ordinary payment settles it.

How a payment is applied

You do not pick which charge a payment settles. The system applies it to the oldest unpaid month first and works forward. That is standard practice and it is what makes the ageing report honest.

Section 07

Taking money — the most important section

Read this even if you read nothing else

There are two different buttons and they mean two completely different things. Using the wrong one is the commonest mistake anyone makes with this system.

ButtonMeansWhat actually happens
Receive payment
on an arrears alert or a ledger row		 The money is in my hand now. A receipt number is issued straight away. The balance drops. The arrears alert disappears. It shows in every report.
Record payment
the button at the top of the ledger		 It is promised but has not arrived — a cheque in the post, a transfer they say they sent. Logged as awaiting funds. No receipt number. No change to any balance or report. Nothing at all until you confirm it.
Why it works this way

Every single figure in this system — balances, arrears, collection rate, projections, the annual report — comes from confirmed receipts only. A payment that never actually turned up can therefore never flatter your numbers. That is a protection, not an obstacle.

Taking a payment that is in hand

  1. Find the account. Quickest routes: the arrears list on the General Dashboard, or the Tenant rent / Owner maintenance tab of the ledger.
  2. Press Receive payment on that row.
  3. Enter the amount, the currency, how they paid, and any reference (cheque number, transfer reference).
  4. Save. The receipt opens automatically. Print it or email it before you close it.
If it refuses the currency

You are receipting in a currency the account is not billed in. Nine times out of ten it is a slip — change it. If the money genuinely came in the other currency you can force it through, but that receipt is then reported separately as a currency mismatch and is not netted off the balance, because the two currencies are never combined. Sort it properly rather than forcing it.

Confirming a payment that has now arrived

  1. Open HOA & Tenants Ledger → Receipts ledger.
  2. Find the entry — it is marked as awaiting funds. Use the search box for the payer, the villa or the reference.
  3. Press Confirm received.
  4. That is the moment the receipt number is issued and the money reaches the balances and the reports.

The same rule governs utility bills and booking deposits: the bill stays unpaid, and the deposit stays expected, until the receipt is confirmed.

Section 08

Receipts

Yes, the system issues receipts by itself. You never write a receipt number. The moment a payment is confirmed as received, the next number in sequence is stamped on it automatically, and the printable receipt opens on screen.

Printing

Press Print. Your browser's print dialog opens. To keep a digital copy instead of paper, choose Save as PDF as the destination.

Emailing

Press Email and a pre-filled draft opens in your own mail program, addressed to the tenant, owner or guest on file. You read it and you press send.

The system never sends mail on its own. It does not have your mail password and nothing leaves without you seeing it. A mail draft cannot carry an attachment, so the receipt details are written into the body of the message. If someone wants a formal copy, save the receipt as a PDF first and attach it yourself.

The Email button is greyed out when there is no email address on file for that person. Add one on their record in the Units Register.

Finding an old receipt

HOA & Tenants Ledger → Receipts ledger. Search by receipt number, payer name, villa or reference, and filter by category and date. Open any of them to view or reprint.

Reprints are honest

A second copy is printed with REPRINT shown on it, and the system counts how many times it has been printed. A voided receipt prints stamped VOID. Nobody can quietly produce a clean duplicate.

Other documents you can print

  • Account statement — on the tenant and owner tabs, each row has a Statement button. A full itemised statement: every charge and payment in date order with a running balance and the closing position.
  • Guest stay receipt — on the Short-Term Bookings list. The guest-facing document (villa, dates, nights, total, deposit, balance due on arrival), which is a different thing from the internal payment receipt.
  • Unit ledger — the full financial history of one villa, including its occupancy history.

Section 09

Fixing a mistake

Nothing in this system is ever deleted. Mistakes are corrected by cancelling the wrong record — visibly, with a reason and your name against it — and entering the right one. That is what an auditor expects to see, and it is why the books can be trusted.

A payment entered wrongly

  1. Find it in the Receipts ledger.
  2. Press Void receipt (called reversing the payment).
  3. Type a real reason. "Error" is useless in six months' time; "entered against Villa 4 in error, belongs to Villa 14" is useful.
  4. Enter the payment again, correctly. A new receipt number is issued.

The voided receipt stays in the ledger, marked VOID, with the reason and your name. It no longer affects any balance.

Cannot be undone

Voiding is one-way. There is no un-void. Be sure before you confirm — and note that voiding needs the Reverse payments permission, so a junior may not have it.

A charge raised in error

Open the charges list for that villa from the ledger and void the charge with a reason. Charges are never deleted either. If you posted a month you did not mean to, void the individual charges — do not try to "unpost" it, there is no such thing.

A tenant's name typed wrongly

When you change a tenant's name the system stops and asks you which of two things you mean, because they are completely different and it cannot tell from the outside:

  • A correction — the name was spelt wrong. Nothing else changes. No new tenancy, no rent raised.
  • A new tenant — somebody different has moved in. The old tenancy is closed and a new one opens, with first and last month's rent billed.

Answer honestly. Choosing "new tenant" for a typo bills two months of rent that nobody owes.

Section 10

Tenants

A villa's record shows the current occupant. The history of everyone who has lived there is kept separately and is never edited away — you can always answer who was in Villa 9 in 2024 and what they were paying.

Moving a tenant in

  1. Open the villa in the Units Register.
  2. Set it to a long-term lease and enter the tenant's name, phone and email.
  3. Enter the monthly rent and the currency, and the day of the month rent is due.
  4. Use Edit tenancy dates to record the date they actually moved in — not the day you did the paperwork.
  5. If there is a fixed lease term, enter the lease end date. Leave it empty for month-to-month.
  6. Save.
What gets billed on move-in

For a tenant starting on or after the date in "The books begin on", recording the move-in date bills two months at once: their first month, and their last month held in advance.

The advance is not a deposit and is not refunded — it pays for their final month. When the tenancy is eventually closed, that month is not charged again. A tenant who stays 14 months is charged exactly 14 months' rent.

For a tenant who moved in before that date, nothing is billed at all. Their tenancy is simply recorded.

Part-month move-ins

A tenant moving in on the 20th is billed the whole month. Rent is not split by days here — that is the policy, not an oversight. The charge says so on its face ("full month; moved in 20 Sep, not prorated") so it never looks like a billing error to whoever reads the statement.

Lease expiry

A lease end date is not the date the tenant left — it is the date the agreement runs to. Leases within 60 days of expiry, and any already lapsed, are flagged on the dashboard and in the Notifications Hub so you have time to renew.

An expired term does not end the tenancy. It carries on month to month at the same rent until it is renewed or notice is served.

Rent increases

Rent is recorded on the tenancy as agreed at the time. Raising the rent for a new tenant does not rewrite what the previous one was charged, so the history stays true.

Moving a tenant out

  1. Open the villa and use Edit tenancy dates.
  2. Enter the date they actually left and tick the box to close the tenancy.
  3. Give the reason.
  4. Save. If an advance was held, their final month is not charged — and if it had already been billed, that charge is voided automatically with the reason recorded.
For tenants who were already here in September 2026

These tenants have no advance recorded in this system, because their first-and-last was collected years ago outside it. When one of them moves out, the system will bill their final month normally — it has nothing to spend.

When that happens, void that final month's rent charge with the reason "covered by last month's rent paid in advance at move-in". Nothing to do now; just do not let it catch you out at the first move-out.

Eviction

When a tenant leaves owing rent, that debt should stop appearing as if it were still collectable. A Manager or Administrator opens the villa and uses Evict tenant on the active tenancy.

This closes the tenancy as an eviction with the date and reason, writes off the unpaid rent as bad debt so it leaves the dashboard and the hub, and frees the villa. Nothing is deleted — the tenancy, every receipt, and the amount written off all stay on the record and in the audit trail, and the eviction shows on the occupancy history with the sum written off.

A Junior or Finance Officer will not see this button.

Section 11

Owners and the HOA maintenance account

Every villa has an owner, and every owner pays a monthly maintenance contribution to the body corporate. This is a separate account from rent, with its own balance, its own currency, and its own due day.

  • Owner maintenance tab on the ledger works exactly like the tenant tab — same balances, same Receive payment, same Statement button.
  • The monthly fee is billed by the same Post month that bills the rent.
  • A special assessment or any other owner charge is raised with Bill a one-off against the Maintenance account.
  • An owner who lives in their own villa still has a maintenance account; they simply have no tenant.

Owners Statements

The Owners Statements screen is a different thing from the account statement. It shows an owner what their villa earned and cost over a period — useful when you manage the villa on their behalf. Pick a date range (last month, this month, last 3 or 6 months, year to date, or a custom range) and print or email it.

Section 12

Short-term guest bookings

Entering a reservation

  1. Open Short-Term Bookings and press New reservation.
  2. Pick the villa and the arrival and departure dates. The system checks availability as you type and will not let you double-book.
  3. Enter the guest's name and contact details, the nightly rate or total stay value, and any deposit taken.
  4. Save.
How nights are counted

A stay occupies the nights from arrival up to but not including departure. So a guest leaving on the 20th and another arriving on the 20th is a perfectly valid same-day changeover, not a clash. The calendar and the availability check both work this way.

Deposits and balances

A deposit follows the same rule as everything else: it stays expected until the receipt is confirmed. Once received it shows in "Deposits held", and the remainder shows in "Balances due on arrival" on the dashboard.

Damage deposits are handled separately on the booking so they are not mixed up with the stay payment.

Guest receipt

Each reservation has a Guest receipt button. This is the document you give the guest — villa, dates, nights, total, deposit paid, balance due on arrival. It is separate from the internal payment receipt kept in the finance ledger.

Airbnb, Booking.com and VRBO calendars

Under System Settings → Calendar feeds you can paste the .ics export link that each platform publishes for a listing, then press Sync now.

Know what you are getting. A platform feed carries booked dates only — no guest contact details and no pricing, because the platforms do not publish them. So:

  • Imported stays arrive with a stay value of zero. You must type in what it is worth.
  • Anything clashing with a booking you already have is skipped, never silently overwritten.
  • Re-syncing updates the dates but leaves everything you typed alone.
Cannot be undone

A cancelled reservation cannot be reinstated. If a guest changes their mind again, create a new reservation.

Section 13

Utilities and expenses

This screen has three tabs, and they hold three different kinds of thing.

Metered utilities

For water and electricity that are metered per villa. Enter the billing period and the reading, and the system works out consumption and what is owed.

A utility bill is unpaid until its receipt is confirmed — the same rule as everywhere else. Settling it from this screen records the payment and issues the receipt.

Expense register

Every bill the property pays: contractors, gardening, security, insurance, repairs, supplies. Record the vendor, the category, the amount, the currency, the date and — where it matters — the due date. An expense can be attached to a specific villa or booked to the property as a whole.

The categories you pick here are what group the expense analysis in the reports and the charts, so use them consistently. Common vendors are kept in a list under System Settings → General so the names stay consistent rather than being retyped four different ways.

Monthly costs

Fixed recurring costs that happen every month without a fresh bill each time. Set them up once and post them monthly rather than retyping them.

Charging a repair back to a tenant

These are two separate acts. Record what the property paid as an expense here. Then, if a tenant is to be charged for it, raise a Bill a one-off against their rent account. One is money out, the other is money owed to you.

Section 14

Repairs and maintenance requests

A repair logged against a villa is tracked as a job, not left as a note. Open the villa in the Units Register and use Maintenance request.

  1. It opens as Reported — somebody said something is broken.
  2. Assign it to a contractor and schedule it.
  3. Move it along as the work progresses.
  4. Close it as Completed or Cancelled, with the date, what was done and what it cost.

When you close it you can raise the cost as a property expense against that villa in the same step. That happens once only, so it can never reach the accounts twice.

Other kinds of activity — a pool booking, a noise complaint, a notice served — are recorded as a note of something that happened. They are not tracked as work with a status, because they are not jobs.

All of these read the same underlying figures, so they cannot contradict each other or the ledger. What differs is the shape and the audience.

Why the Annual Report is a form and not a document editor

Every figure, table and chart is generated from the books into fixed slots. The only things you type are the eight narrative boxes. This is deliberate: nobody can type over a number, so the arithmetic in the printed report is always the arithmetic in the books.

Your wording carries forward year to year — opening next year's report starts from this year's text with every figure recalculated, rather than from a blank page.

A note on projections

Financial Projections deliberately excludes rent and guest income. That money belongs to individual proprietors, not to the body corporate, so putting it in a strata projection would be wrong. It is not a bug.

Section 16

Bank reconciliation

Every other figure in this system comes from the property's own records. That makes them consistent with each other, which is not the same as true. A transfer that was promised and never sent, a cheque that bounced, and cash that was receipted but never banked all look identical to money that actually arrived.

This screen is the only place the books are checked against something outside the books. It is a manual tick-off, on purpose — it works with a paper statement, a PDF, or your banking app open on a phone.

  1. Open Bank Reconciliation.
  2. Choose the period and the currency. One period, one currency at a time.
  3. Put the bank statement beside the screen and work down the list, ticking each receipt you can actually see on the statement.
  4. Drive the figure at the top of the screen to zero. That number is the whole point of the exercise.
  5. Print the completed reconciliation for the file.

Anything left unticked at the end is exactly what you want to know about: money the books think you have and the bank does not.

Section 17

Staff accounts and who can do what

There are four roles. Only an Administrator can create or change staff accounts.

RoleBroadly
AdministratorEverything, always. Also the only role that can manage staff accounts.
ManagerDay-to-day authority including the settings, evictions and voiding receipts.
Finance OfficerThe money work, without the veto powers.
Junior OfficerCollects rent and issues receipts. Cannot evict or void.

The individual permissions

Under System Settings → Role permissions you can switch each of these on or off per role:

  • Record payments & issue receipts
  • Create guest reservations
  • Edit guest reservations
  • Cancel reservations & issue refunds
  • Edit the unit register (rates, owners, tenants)
  • Record utility meter readings
  • Record property expenses
  • Post monthly rent & maintenance charges
  • Reverse payments & void receipts
  • Modify settled ledger records

Recording payments is granted to every role by default and for a good reason: when nobody senior is on site the junior officer still has to run the day, and collecting rent is the day. It is a permission rather than an open door so it can still be withdrawn if you ever want to.

These are real locks

Permissions are enforced on the server for every single request, not merely hidden on the screen. Someone who knows their way around a browser cannot get past them.

Adding somebody

  1. System Settings → Staff accounts.
  2. Create the account with their real name, a username and a role.
  3. Give them the starting password in person. They are made to change it at first sign-in.

When somebody leaves

Disable their account the same day. Do not delete it and do not hand their login to their replacement — every receipt they ever issued is stamped with their name, and that record needs to stay meaningful.

The Audit Trail

Every action is recorded with who did it, when, and what changed from what to what. It is read-only — nobody can edit or clear it, including an Administrator. When there is a question about who did something, this is the answer.

Section 18

Backups and getting your records back

All the records live in a single file on the HP called db.json. It is written carefully — a crash or a power cut cannot leave it half-written, and two people saving at the same moment cannot overwrite each other. A dated copy is kept automatically every day, and the last 30 are retained.

Those daily copies are not enough on their own

They sit on the same disk as the file they protect. That survives a bad edit. It does not survive a failed hard drive, a stolen computer, or someone deleting the folder. You need a copy somewhere else.

Set up an off-machine backup (do this once)

  1. Plug a USB stick or external drive into the HP.
  2. Go to System Settings → Backup & recovery.
  3. Paste the folder path — on Windows something like E:\Villaggio-Backups.
  4. Press Save and back up now.

The path is tested by actually writing to it, so you find out immediately if it is wrong. A copy is taken there and then, and another follows every day. If the drive is later unplugged, the path is remembered so you never retype it, the copy on the HP still succeeds, and the screen tells you plainly that the external folder could not be written to rather than quietly showing nothing.

Take a snapshot before anything risky

System Settings → Backup & recovery → Download a snapshot gives you a single file containing every record. Passwords and sessions are stripped out, so it is safe to email to yourself or drop in cloud storage.

Do this before a software update, before a bulk change, and at the end of every month.

Restoring

  1. Close the program on the HP (close the console window).
  2. Copy the backup file you want over db.json.
  3. Start the program again.

Backups are plain text — you can open one and read it before you restore it, to be sure it is the one you want.

It refuses to start rather than damage your records

If db.json is ever unreadable, the program will not start. That is a safety feature, not a failure: it would rather stop than replace your records with an empty file. Restore a backup and start it again.

Updating the program

Your records and the program are completely separate files. Updating replaces only the program and never touches your data.

  1. On the HP, close the current program.
  2. Put the new program file in the same folder, replacing the old one.
  3. Start it. It checks its own records on start and prints the result — on a healthy database it says "no contradictions found".
  4. On every other device, press Ctrl+Shift+R once to pick up the new screens.

Section 19

When something looks wrong

Work down this list. Nearly everything is one of these.

"Nobody owes anything, but they definitely do"

The month has not been posted. Go to the ledger and press Post month. Remember: nothing is owed until it is billed.

"I entered a payment and the balance did not change"

You used Record payment rather than Receive payment, so it is sitting as awaiting funds. Open the Receipts ledger, find it, and press Confirm received. See Section 07.

"A payment shows in the ledger but the balance ignores it"

It was receipted in the wrong currency, so it is deliberately not netted off — a yellow note at the top of the ledger names the accounts affected. Void that receipt and enter it in the right currency.

"The totals do not add up across villas"

Check whether you are looking at a mix of JMD and USD villas. They are never added together, anywhere. Each currency has its own total.

"A tenant who has been here for years is showing a huge debt"

"The books begin on" is not set, or is set to the wrong date. Go to System Settings → General and set it to 1 September 2026. Then void the charges that were wrongly raised against months before that date.

"Somebody is showing as late and they are not"

Check that villa's rent due day in the Units Register. If it says 1 and they actually pay on the 15th, fix it. Note that charges already raised keep the due date they were given.

"An imported Airbnb booking has no value on it"

Correct and expected. Platform calendars publish dates only — never prices or guest details. Type the stay value in yourself.

"I cannot see a button somebody else has"

Your role does not have that permission. An Administrator can adjust it under Role permissions, or do the action for you. Evicting and voiding are restricted on purpose.

"Reconnecting to the server…"

Wait. It retries by itself and cannot double-record. If it persists: check the HP is on and its console window is still open; check the network. If a save does eventually fail, your form keeps everything you typed — just press save again once the banner clears. Nothing you entered is lost.

"The screens look like the old version"

Press Ctrl+Shift+R. Your browser is showing a cached copy of the screens. Balances are always fetched fresh, so this never affects the figures — only the layout.

Still stuck

Open the Audit Trail and look at what was actually done, by whom, and when. It records the before and after of every change, and it usually answers the question in under a minute.

Section 20

Things that cannot be undone

These are financial controls, not limitations. Each one exists so the records cannot be quietly rewritten.

One-way, no exceptions
  • Voiding a receipt. It stays in the ledger marked VOID with the reason and your name. To correct a payment: void it, then enter a new one.
  • Charges are never deleted, only voided with a reason.
  • A cancelled reservation cannot be reinstated. Create a new one.
  • Receipt numbers are sequential and never reused — including after restoring a backup.
  • Reprints are marked as reprints and counted.
  • The audit trail cannot be edited or cleared by anyone, including an Administrator.
